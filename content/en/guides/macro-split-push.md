At the same mechanical level, one team still ends up winning more often than the other. The difference is the macro: being in the right place, at the right time, with the right number of players. It requires no reflexes or special heroes, just attention.

## Watch the minimap

The minimap is the first source of information in the game. A glance every few seconds is enough, as long as you're looking for the right things:

- **how many opponents are visible**, and where;
- **which waves advance**, and towards which turret;
- **which objective is approaching**: Turtle, Lord, opposing buff.

An opponent who disappears from the mini-map isn't anywhere. If he was in mid lane five seconds ago, he's within a radius you can estimate. It is this estimate that tells you if a target is safe.

## Trading objectives

A game is not played objective by objective: it is played in exchanges. If the opponent takes the Turtle while your team takes two turrets, the exchange is in your favor.

- **When an objective is lost in advance**, take another on the other side of the map rather than fighting four against five.
- **When you take an objective**, ask yourself what the opponent can take in that time.
- **An exterior turret is worth less than an interior turret**: the second brings more gold to the team and opens up the enemy jungle more.

The [map guide](/map) reminds you where each turret and camp are, and the [objective timeline](/tools/timer) tells you in advance when the exchange will come up.

## Split pushing: pushing alone

Split push consists of pushing one lane alone while the other four play elsewhere. The opponent must then choose: send someone to defend and play four against four on the other side, or let the turret fall.

It works when three conditions are met:

1. **The hero who pushes wins the duel** against the one who comes to defend, or can flee without dying.
2. **The rest of the team does not fight**: it holds a defensive position, under a turret or near an objective, and refuses combat when outnumbered.
3. **Waves are managed**: the lane your team is in is pushed, and this wave maintains the pressure while you are elsewhere.

Good split push heroes quickly take down a turret and have enough to escape. [Sun](/heroes/sun) turns into a staff, hides and reappears further away with Swift Exchange; [Zilong](/heroes/zilong) gains movement speed and ignores slows with Supreme Warrior. [Masha](/heroes/masha), she only has one rush (Claw Strike) to leave: she must monitor the mini-map and leave the lane before the arrival of the defenders.

## When to Avoid Split Push

Split push becomes an error in three situations:

- **the Lord is available** and the opponent can take it with five while you push alone;
- **your team doesn't know you're pushing** and engages in combat without you;
- **the strongest opposing hero in a duel** no longer appears on the minimap: he may be waiting for you.

## Late game: the Lord and the waves

After about fifteen minutes, respawn times are long and a single lost fight can open the base. The macro becomes simpler, but each error costs more:

- only fight with the Lord or a turret on hand;
- always push the wave before an objective, so that it arrives at the same time as you;
- never leave a player alone in the middle of the map without vision.

The [weekly meta report](/meta) also shows which heroes are going up or down: when the split push heroes are strong, you have to defend your lanes with more rigor.

## The most common mistakes

- Group together as a group of five in Mid lane, letting two waves push to the sides.
- Abandoning a goal without taking anything in exchange.
- Go into a split push without warning while the team is fighting: it plays the fight four against five.
- Run towards a distant fight instead of taking the turret in front of you.
