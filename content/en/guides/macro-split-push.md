Two teams of the same mechanical level do not win as often as each other. The difference is the macro: the ability to be in the right place, at the right time, with the right number of players. It requires neither reflexes nor precise heroes, only attention.

## Watch the minimap

The minimap is the first source of information in the game. A glance every few seconds is enough, as long as you're looking for the right things:

- **how many opponents are visible**, and where;
- **which waves advance**, and towards which turret;
- **which objective is approaching**: Turtle, Lord, opposing buff.

An opponent who disappears from the minimap isn't anywhere. If he was in mid lane five seconds ago, he's within a radius you can estimate. It is this estimate that tells you if a target is safe.

## Trading objectives

A game is not played objective by objective: it is played in exchanges. If the opponent takes the Turtle while your team takes two turrets, the exchange is in your favor.

- **When an objective is lost in advance**, take another on the other side of the map rather than fighting four against five.
- **When you take an objective**, ask yourself what the opponent can take in that time.
- **An exterior turret is worth less than an interior turret**: the order of destruction counts for access to the opponent's jungle.

The [map guide](/map) reminds you where each turret and each camp is located, and the [objective chronology](/tools/timer) allows you to know in advance when the exchange will occur.

## Split pushing: pushing alone

Split pushing involves pushing a lane alone while the rest of the team stands elsewhere. It forces the opponent to choose: send someone to defend, and play four against four elsewhere, or drop the turret.

It works when three conditions are met:

1. **The hero who pushes wins the duel** against the one who comes to defend, or can flee without dying.
2. **The rest of the team does not fight**: they hold a defensive position, under a turret or near an objective, without accepting four-on-five combat.
3. **Waves are managed**: a lane pushed in front of your team keeps the pressure on while you are elsewhere.

Heroes designed for this destroy a turret quickly and leave the lane easily: [Sun](/heroes/sun), [Zilong](/heroes/zilong) or [Masha](/heroes/masha) are known examples.

## When not to split push

Split push becomes an error in three situations:

- **the Lord is in play** and the opponent can take it with five while you push alone;
- **your team doesn't know you're pushing** and engages in combat without you;
- **the strongest opposing hero in a duel** no longer appears on the minimap: he may be waiting for you.

## Late game: the Lord and the waves

After about fifteen minutes, the deaths last a long time and a single lost fight can open the base. The macro then becomes simpler, and more severe:

- only fight with the Lord or a turret on hand;
- always push the wave before an objective, so that it arrives at the same time as you;
- never leave a player alone in the middle of the map without vision.

The [weekly meta report](/meta) also shows which heroes are moving up or down: a meta rich in split push heroes calls for more discipline on lane defense.

## The most common mistakes

- Group together as a group of five in mid lane, letting two waves push to the sides.
- Abandoning a goal without taking anything in exchange.
- Split push while the teamfights, without warning: it's a four-on-five fight.
- Run towards a distant fight instead of taking the turret in front of you.
