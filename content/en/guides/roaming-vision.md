MLBB has no vision tags to place: vision comes from heroes, minions, turrets and some card effects. Whoever sees the opponent without being seen chooses the fight. This is why the roamer, always on the move, weighs more than any other player on the vision of his team.

## What gives vision

- **Heroes, minions, and turrets** reveal what's around them, but not the inside of a bush.
- **The bushes** hide a hero as long as no enemy has entered them or revealed them through an effect.
- **The Lithowanderer**, a small river creature, spawns a stone sentinel that signals enemies to the team that finished it off.
- **Certain skills** reveal an area or a hero: at the start of the game, the information they give is often worth more than their damage.

The [map guide](/map) locates the bushes, the river and the camps, and gives the times of each creature.

## The first two minutes

The roamer starts the game where his team is most vulnerable: often with the jungler, to protect his first buff from an invasion, then in Gold lane or mid for the first wave.

The key moment comes around 1:45. The Turtle spawns at 2:00 near the EXP lane, and the team holding the river a few seconds before it spawns almost always takes it. The roamer must be there before the opposing jungler, not after.

## Rotations: when to leave your lane

A roamer never stays in one place for long. The principle is simple: be where something is going to happen.

1. **After each pushed wave** in Gold lane, the roamer can leave: the opposing wave no longer directly threatens the shooter.
2. **Before each objective**, he takes the river fifteen to twenty seconds before spawning to check the bushes around.
3. **After a won fight**, it accompanies the push: a turret or an opposing buff brings more than a return to base.
4. **When an allied lane is in difficulty**, he only goes there if he can arrive in time; otherwise, he puts the pressure elsewhere.

The classic mistake is to stay glued to the marksman for ten minutes. The marksman needs help during the first waves and in fights, not to farm a wave already won.

## Hold a bush

Positioning yourself in a bush is not enough: you need the right bush, at the right time.

- **In front of an objective**, hold the bush through which the opponent must pass to reach it, not the one next to the Turtle.
- **In the opposing jungle**, a bush near a buff that will reappear tells you: if the opposing jungler does not come and take it, it is because he is elsewhere.
- **Before entering an unknown bush**, reveal it with a skill or let a minion pass through it, especially with a fragile hero.

A bush held by an engagement tank like [Tigreal](/heroes/tigreal), [Atlas](/heroes/atlas) or [Franco](/heroes/franco) constantly weighs on the opponent: he hesitates to advance, and these seconds of hesitation are often enough to take the objective.

## Vision serving objectives

Before each objective, vision is mainly used to answer a question: where are the adversaries? The roamer and the jungler must be able to answer them together.

- **Before the Turtle or the Lord**, count the opponents visible on the mini-map. At least three visible, far from the objective: we can start. Two or less, or visible opponents near the objective: we check the bushes first, or we wait to learn more.
- **During the objective**, the roamer places himself between the objective and the path by which the opponent is arriving, not on the objective itself.
- **After an objective**, he guards the river while the team retreats or pushes.

The [objective timer](/tools/timer) helps anticipate these moments, and the [team analysis](/tools/team) shows if your composition has what it takes to engage, protect or reveal.

## The most common mistakes

- Enter alone into an opposing bush with fragile support: the roamer is the scout, not prey.
- Follow the marksman the whole game and never prepare the river before the Turtle.
- Report too late that an opponent has disappeared from the map: ten seconds later, the information is no longer useful.
- Leave the Gold lane while the opposing wave arrives at the turret: the shooter loses his wave, sometimes his turret.
