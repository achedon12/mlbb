You don't move up in rank by winning a spectacular game, but by winning a few more games than you lose, over hundreds of games. Ranked rewards consistency: an average player who never makes a big mistake rises faster than a brilliant but irregular player. The [rank system](/ranks) details the tiers and what changes from one to the next.

## A main role, a backup role

No one masters all five positions. In ranked, the best strategy is simple:

- **a main role**, the one you play most often and that you understand best;
- **a backup role**, for games where your position is already taken;
- **a playable hero on each other position**, to never block the draft.

Positions that few players want, like roam, are a good entry point: you almost always get them, and a good roamer influences the entire map. Each position has its guide: [gold lane](/guides/gold-lane), [exp lane](/guides/exp-lane), [mid lane](/guides/mid-lane), [jungle](/guides/jungle) and [roam](/guides/roam).

## A small pool of heroes

Three heroes on his main role are more than enough. Each hero has his duels, his timings and his items: playing fifteen means mastering none of them.

To choose these three heroes:

1. **a strong hero in the current patch**, that the [tier list](/tier-list) or the [tier list of your rank](/tier-list/mythic) places at the top;
2. **a hero that you like to play**, because it is the one on which you will progress the most;
3. **a backup hero** who responds to the most frequent counters of your first two choices.

A hero who is banned almost every game at your rank should not be your only choice: check his ban rate in the [statistics table](/statistics).

## The mentality: play the next game

Ranked wears patience more than reflexes. A few rules protect your points:

- **stop after two defeats in a row**: the third game is often played angry, and it is the one that costs the most;
- **do not reply in the chat**: each message written is a second where we do not look at the map;
- **a game is never lost before the inhibitor**: endgame Lords return situations that seemed lost;
- **play rested**: hours of fatigue are when small errors accumulate.

## Learn from your defeats

A defeat teaches more than a victory, provided you look at it honestly. After a lost game, ask yourself just one question: what decision did I make that I would not make again? Not that of your teammates: yours.

Game replays are the most useful tool for this. Look at the minimap at the time of your death: most of the time, the information that would have prevented it was there.

## Measure your progress

Win rate is the only metric that matters over time, and it moves slowly. The [win rate calculator](/tools/win-rate) says how many wins in a row it takes to reach a goal: useful for setting a realistic goal rather than a number that would require a hundred wins in a row.

Also compare your heroes to their average: a hero with 50% wins in the current patch is a good choice if you play him above this average, a bad one if you play him below.

## The most common mistakes

- Change main hero each patch following the last video.
- Continue the games after a series of defeats.
- Take a role you don’t know to “try out” in a ranked format.
- Judge your game by score rather than objectives and gold earned.
